## Look beyond the product features

ResumeAI launched with a package-based model: customers bought bundles of product usage. We saw strong free usage, but free-to-paid conversion remained poor. People were trying the product without moving into a paid relationship at the rate we needed.

That made the way we charged for access a product question. I investigated pricing friction through customer interviews and competitive analysis, looking for a model that better matched the value customers could get from continued use.

## Change what the customer is buying

I led the transition from fixed-usage packages to a subscription with unlimited access. The change gave paying customers ongoing access instead of a finite bundle of uses, and we positioned the offer around that longer-term value.

This was a change to the business model as well as the offer. We moved from selling individual packages toward an ongoing customer relationship, making retention and customer lifetime value central to understanding whether the new model worked.

## Follow the value beyond the first purchase

I tracked customer lifetime value in Stripe for paying customers, from becoming a paid customer through cancellation. After the transition, that figure rose from about $17 under the package model to over $100—roughly six times the previous value. Average paid subscription duration stabilized at three months or more.

Those results gave us a broader view of the business than the initial free-to-paid conversion alone. They showed how much value a paying customer generated and how long the paid relationship lasted.

My contribution combined customer research, analysis of the existing pricing friction, and ownership of the monetization change. The work connected the offer customers encountered to the business outcome we needed to understand: the value of a continuing customer relationship.
