## Scope

This exploration connects my interest in information theory and language models with a practical question about career documents. A longer work history can describe decisions, responsibilities, constraints, and outcomes in detail. A résumé must choose what to carry forward into a much shorter form.

I want to examine that choice directly: which information survives the compression, which information disappears, and how the resulting document might support a reader’s understanding of someone’s experience. The focus is on the information in the document, without assuming that a shorter or more polished version is necessarily a better representation.

## Questions I want to investigate

- Which details are preserved consistently across different summaries of the same work history?
- What happens to context, qualifications, and the distinction between individual work and team outcomes?
- How does tailoring a résumé to a role change what is emphasized or omitted?
- What would a useful measure of information loss need to account for?

## Possible approach

A proposed first step is to define a small set of clearly described career accounts using synthetic or explicitly authorized material. I could compare shorter versions against their source accounts and record preserved details, omissions, and unsupported additions. A further comparison could vary the target role or space available.

The examples, evaluation criteria, and summarization methods remain to be chosen. This is a proposed approach, not a description of a completed dataset or study.

## What I hope to learn

I want to develop a more precise way to discuss faithful compression in a familiar workflow. Any eventual brief would distinguish observations from judgments about what a particular reader needs. This draft establishes the questions; it does not report findings or hiring outcomes.
